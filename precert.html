Welcome to the Pre-Cert Vipco Collection

If you are collecting VIPCO, this is where the story begins. Before VIPCO became synonymous with the infamous British “Video Nasties” era, the company was already finding its feet in the exciting and largely unregulated world of home video. Founded by Mike Lee, VIPCO, the Video Instant Picture Company, emerged at a time when VHS and other home video formats were still in their infancy. The early catalogue was surprisingly eclectic, ranging from exploitation and horror to sex films, educational material and other unusual titles. Among the earliest known VIPCO releases is the now seemingly lost Introducing Badminton, a 1977 instructional film featuring British badminton champion Gillian Gilks. Today, the tape is considered an extremely elusive piece of VIPCO history, and may well be lost. From these modest beginnings, VIPCO would quickly move towards the horror and exploitation films that would define its reputation. The company's catalogue soon included titles such as Zombie Flesh Eaters, The Driller Killer and The Toolbox Murders. VIPCO's willingness to market these films aggressively and, crucially, to offer versions that had previously been unavailable or heavily cut placed the company directly at the centre of Britain's rapidly growing home video controversy.

When Zombie Flesh Eaters first appeared on VIPCO in 1980, it was the cut version that had previously been passed for theatrical exhibition. But in 1981 VIPCO went further, releasing what it advertised as the “Strong Uncut Version.” That release would become one of the most notorious examples of the so-called Video Nasties and help cement VIPCO's place in British video history. What followed was the Video Nasties panic of the early 1980s. With no specific legislation governing home video when the market first emerged, distributors could release material that had never been submitted to the BBFC, including uncut versions of films that had been censored for cinema. The resulting controversy eventually led to the Video Recordings Act 1984, bringing compulsory BBFC certification to UK home video. Mike Lee and VIPCO would become important figures in this extraordinary period. The company survived the crackdown and, after the introduction of the new certification regime, entered a very different era of home video. Films that VIPCO had once proudly promoted as uncut could now have to be submitted to the BBFC and altered to meet the new requirements.

These early tapes represent VIPCO before certification changed everything before the familiar BBFC certificates, before the post 1984 cuts, and before VIPCO's later reputation was built around re-releasing the infamous titles of the Video Nasties era. For collectors, the Pre-Cert tapes are the beginning of the VIPCO story. Start here. Explore the earliest releases, the cardboard sleeves, the alternative formats, the odd catalogue numbers and the elusive titles that existed before VIPCO became a household name among British horror fans.
